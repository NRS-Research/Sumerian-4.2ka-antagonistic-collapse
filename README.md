Climate stress, inter-polity conflict and societal collapse: quantifying the ~116-year lag in 4.2 ka Sumer using an antagonistic dynamics model

Replication data and code for the manuscript

This repository provides the complete reproducible dataset and numerical simulation code for the Antagonistic Dynamics Model, which explains the centennial-scale lag between climatic stress peaks and societal collapse across Bronze Age civilizations during the 4.2 ka event. The Sumerian civilization serves as the core quantitative case study.

---
Research Background

The 4.2 ka event​ (ca. 2310–2020 BCE) was one of the most severe global aridification episodes of the Holocene. Archaeological records show that major Bronze Age civilizations — Sumer, Old Kingdom Egypt, the Indus Valley, and Minoan Crete — experienced systemic collapse with lags of 100–200 years​ after the climatic aridity peak, rather than immediately. Our model quantifies a ~116-year collapse lag​ for the Sumerian core case (consistent with the archaeologically observed collapse at 2004 BCE within chronological and parametric uncertainties) and confirms that this centennial-scale delay is a structural emergent property of the antagonistic dynamics between climatic stress and societal resilience.

This study proposes an antagonistic dynamics model​ to explain this lag phenomenon through a three-stage dynamical sequence:

1.Pressure buildup: Climatic stress gradually erodes productive capacity

2.Antagonistic amplification: Rising resource competition drives internal militarization and inter-polity conflict

3.Resilience depletion: Systemic resilience decays exponentially until collapse

The model is formalized as a system of two coupled differential equations:

$$
\frac{dW}{dt} = r - k \cdot \gamma
$$

$$
\frac{d\gamma}{dt} = \alpha \cdot s(t) \cdot W - \beta \cdot \gamma
$$

where:
- $W(t)$ is systemic resilience surplus
- $\gamma(t)$ is antagonistic investment (conflict level)
- $r$ is the baseline growth rate
- $k$ is the resilience consumption rate per unit conflict
- $\alpha$ is the conflict sensitivity to climatic stress
- $\beta$ is the conflict decay rate
- $s(t)$ is normalized climatic pressure

Repository Contents
Directory	Description

`data/`	Normalized time-series datasets and raw proxy records

`code/`	Python scripts for model simulation, index calculation, and sensitivity analysis

`docs/`	Detailed documentation on data construction, source tracing, and reliability verification

`figures/`	Publication-quality figures generated by the scripts

All data are aggregated into 50-year time slices covering 2400–1900 BCE and normalized to the [0, 1] interval via min-max scaling, consistent with the ±30-year dating uncertainty standard for Bronze Age archaeological contexts.
